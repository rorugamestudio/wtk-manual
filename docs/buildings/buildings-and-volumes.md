# Buildings and Volumes

A :wtk-component-building: **Building** turns a closed footprint into a building. Its **volumes** say how many floors go where, and the **Volume Wall** assets they use say what each side of the building looks like.

## :lucide-pen-tool: Creating a building

1. In the :wtk-buildings: **Buildings** module, set **Draw Creates** to :wtk-component-building: **Building**.
2. Click **Edit Buildings**.
3. Hold ++shift++ and **click** to place the first corner, then **click** to place the others. **Drag** while placing a corner to pull out its handles and curve the walls next to it.
4. **Click** the first corner again, or **right-click**, to close the footprint.

The building generates right away, using the **Building Rules** set under **New Building Defaults**.

!!! note "While drawing"
    - A footprint needs at least three corners. Finishing with fewer removes it.
    - ++esc++ throws away the footprint you're drawing, and also stops **Edit Buildings**.
    - Shift-click starts a new building only when no building is selected. With a building or block selected, it adds the new outline to that object's spline instead, and a building only uses the first outline of its spline. Click an empty spot first to clear the selection.

To edit a footprint later, click **Edit Buildings** and move its knots like any [spline](../splines/spline-container.md#editing-knots). Press ++esc++ to stop editing.

You can also add the **Building** component to an object yourself (**Add Component > World Toolkit > Buildings > Building**). It picks up a **Spline Container** on the same object. Then give it volumes or a **Rules Override**.

## :lucide-sliders-horizontal: Building settings

The top of the inspector sums up the last generation and lists any problems (see [Checking the result](#checking-the-result)). Changing a setting regenerates the building. To build it again by hand, select it and click **Regenerate** in the Buildings panel.

**Spline Container**
:   The footprint: the first spline of this container.

**Explicit Footprint**
:   A list of corner points, in the building's local space. With three or more points it replaces the spline, and its walls are always straight. Buildings generated by a [block](building-blocks.md) use it for their lot.

**Rules Override**
:   When set, the building takes its volumes from these [Building Rules](building-rules.md) instead of its own, which are hidden. Floors and subfloors come from the rules unless they're locked here, and editing the rules regenerates the building. **New** creates a rules asset and assigns it. Expand the field to edit the rules right here.

**Volumes**
:   The building's own volumes, used when there's no **Rules Override**. See [Volumes](#volumes).

**Floors** and **Subfloors**
:   How many floors above ground, and how many below (basements, foundations). They normally come from the rules. Enable **Lock Floors** or **Lock Subfloors** to keep this building's own value.

**Auto Bake After Generation**
:   After each generation, combines the generated pieces into a single mesh on a **Baked Mesh** child object, with a Mesh Collider and a :wtk-component-building-baked-mesh-output: **Building Baked Mesh Output** component. It's lighter to render. The separate pieces are put away in the [pool](index.md#the-buildings-panel). On by default.

**Baked Mesh Source Asset**
:   A mesh asset that each bake writes into, so the baked mesh is stored in your project instead of in the scene.

**Randomize Seed** and **Seed**
:   Rules can be random: which wall goes where, which pieces a slot uses, how many floors. The seed keeps the result stable. Change it to get a different variation from the same rules. With **Randomize Seed** on, the building rolls a new seed every time rules are applied to it, and the **Seed** field is locked.

To turn a building's current setup into a reusable asset, right-click the Building component and choose **Create Building Rules From Current Setup...**. See [Building Rules](building-rules.md#creating-rules).

### :lucide-arrow-up-down: Floor handle

While the Buildings module is active, every building shows a small handle above its top:

- **Drag** it up or down to add or remove floors.
- **Double-click** it to turn **Lock Floors** on or off. The handle is green while the floors are unlocked and red while they're locked.

On a building with a **Rules Override**, dragging locks its floors, so the count you dragged to sticks.

## :lucide-boxes: Volumes

Each volume is a block of floors with its own walls and roof.

**Key**
:   A name other volumes use to stack on top of this one.

**Foundation**
:   Foundation volumes grow **downward** from their ground zero and use the building's subfloor count.

**Ground Zero Volume** and **Ground Zero Floor**
:   Where this volume starts. With no ground zero volume, it starts at the footprint base. Pick another volume's key to stack on it, and **Ground Zero Floor** to choose at which floor: 0 is its base, 1 the top of its first floor. Negative values count from the top: -1 is the top of the last floor.
:   The field warns when no volume has the chosen key (the volume then starts at the footprint base), or when volumes stack on each other in a loop (the volume isn't generated until the loop is broken).

**Floor Count**
:   Floors in this volume. 0 uses the building's floor count, or its subfloor count for foundations.

**Start Floor** and **Height Offset**
:   Floors skipped from ground zero before the first floor (measured with this volume's first floor height, and downward for foundations), and an extra height in meters.

**Terrain Level**
:   On sloped ground, where the base sits between the lowest (0) and highest (1) corner of the footprint. Ignored when a ground zero volume is set.

**Footprint Spline**
:   A footprint of its own, from a spline in the scene. Leave it empty to use the **Explicit Footprint** below, or else the building's footprint.

**Explicit Footprint**
:   Corner points in the building's local space, used when **Footprint Spline** is empty. [Building Rules](building-rules.md) keep volume footprints this way, since an asset can't point at a spline in the scene.

**Walls**
:   The walls placed along the footprint sides. See [Walls](#walls).

**Roof**
:   The [roof](roofs.md) on the last floor. Foundations ignore it. **New** creates a roof asset, and expanding the field lets you edit the roof right here.

**Ground Zero Floor**, **Start Floor** and **Height Offset** are ranges: turn on **Random** to enter a minimum and a maximum, and each building picks a value in between, using its seed.

!!! warning "Overlapping volumes"
    Volumes aren't cut against each other. Where two volumes overlap at the same height, both build their walls inside the other and their mouldings z-fight. Make a projection part of one footprint, or stack the volumes instead. The building inspector warns when this happens.

## :lucide-brick-wall: Walls

A **Volume Wall** asset (**Assets > Create > World Toolkit > Buildings > Volume Wall**) describes what goes along a footprint side. It's a list of **rows** stacked floor by floor, plus an optional **Corner**.

Editing a wall regenerates every building in the open scenes that uses it. With many buildings, the Scene view shows **Regenerating buildings** and a count while it works through them: selected buildings first, then the ones closest to the camera.

### :lucide-boxes: Starting from a kit of prefabs

If you have wall, window and door prefabs but no wall asset yet, World Toolkit can build a first **Volume Wall** from them:

1. Select the prefabs in the Project window.
2. Choose **Assets > Create > World Toolkit > Buildings > Volume Wall From Selected Prefabs**. It's also in the Project window's right-click menu, under **Create**.

The prefabs are sorted by what their names contain:

| Name contains | Used for |
|---|---|
| `door` | A **ground** row, one floor high, with the doors placed once. |
| `window` or `win_` | An **upper** row for all the other floors, with the windows distributed along each side. |
| `corner` | The wall's corner, if the prefab has a vertical cut at its middle. Without one, it counts as a plain wall piece. |
| anything else | Plain wall pieces. The first one (or the first window or door, without any) fills each row: it's stretched on both sides of the doors and windows, so any side length is covered. |

Without a corner prefab, the wall gets a flat procedural corner in the material of its first wall piece. Prefabs without a mesh are skipped.

The new wall is saved next to the first prefab, named after what the prefab names have in common, and selected. Open it to tune the rows, then give it to a volume's sides (see [Assigning walls to sides](#assigning-walls-to-sides)).

### The wall inspector

- At the top, the inspector lists what the generator would skip: rows without slots or items, slots without pieces, pieces without a mesh, chances of 0, corners that can't be built. **Click** an issue to jump to its field.
- **Floor Rows** shows which row each floor gets for a number of **Floors**, top floor first.
- The **Wall Preview** at the bottom of the inspector builds a single face of the wall, with no corners. Its toolbar has **Iso** (orthographic camera), **Wire** (wireframe), **W** (the face width in meters), **Floors**, **Seed** with **Reroll**, **Grid** (four seeds side by side) and **Reset**. Drag in the preview to orbit, scroll to zoom.

### Assigning walls to sides

A volume's **Walls** list decides which wall each footprint side gets. Side 0 runs from knot 0 to knot 1, and so on.

**Chance**
:   The chance this wall takes its turn. On a failed roll, the next wall is tried.

**Max Uses**
:   How many sides this wall can cover. 0 is unlimited.

**Explicit Knot Start**
:   Starts this wall at a specific footprint knot, and it covers the sides from there until the next explicit wall. Use -1 to take turns with the other -1 walls instead.

Sides that no wall can take get the first wall.

### Rows

A row is one band of the wall, one floor high. Rows are listed from the bottom up (foundations go from the top down):

**Name**
:   A label for the row, shown in issues and in the Scene view overlay.

**Max Floors**
:   How many floors this row covers. Rows set to 0 share evenly the floors the other rows leave. For example, a *ground* row (1), an *upper* row (0) and a *cornice* row (1) give a five-floor volume one ground floor, three upper floors and the cornice on top. If every row has a **Max Floors**, the floors above them get no wall: set one row to 0 to fill the rest.

**Corner**
:   A corner used only on this row's floors. See [Corners](#corners).

Each floor is as tall as the tallest row any wall puts on it, so corners stay aligned. A shorter row leaves a gap above it, and the building inspector says so.

Mouldings count as floors: a procedural plinth or cornice row takes a floor of its own. Count those rows when you set floor counts. A box with a plinth, one storey and a cornice needs three floors.

#### Prefab rows

A prefab row is made of **Slots**, laid out one after another from the start of the side (after the corner, if there is one). Each slot holds **Modular Pieces**: prefabs such as a window, a door or a plain panel, each with a **Chance** of being considered, rolled per wall and floor.

**Placement**
:   **Single** places the slot's pieces once. **Distribute** repeats them along the side as many times as they fit, leaving room for the **Single** slots after it.

**Stretching**
:   **Stretch To Fit** scales the slot's pieces along the side so the row fills it exactly. Without a stretching slot, the length left over stays open at the end of the side.

**Chance**
:   The chance for the slot to be used, rolled per wall and floor. Below 1, the whole slot can be left out.

**Max Pieces Count**
:   The most pieces this slot places on each floor, across all walls. 0 is unlimited.

!!! tip "Modelling pieces"
    - Model each piece with its front facing **+Z**: that side faces out of the building.
    - A piece takes as much of the side as its bounds are wide along **X**, and sits on the row's base. A prefab row is as tall as its tallest piece.
    - A **Single** slot that doesn't fit what's left of the side is left out, and the building inspector says where. Shorter pieces or a **Stretch To Fit** filler slot avoid it.

The piece field warns when a prefab has no mesh (it still reserves a 1 m box but renders nothing) or no width along X.

#### Procedural rows

Generated shapes with a **Height** in meters, made of **Items**:

**Wall** items
:   Extrude a 2D **Profile** along the side, for cornices, bands and ledges. **Offset Y** and **Offset Normal** move the profile up and away from the wall. **Smooth Shading Angle** shades the edges between faces smooth when the faces meet at less than this angle, and sharp above it.
:   Where a wall item stops, at a corner or where the next side uses another wall, its ends are closed back to the wall, so mouldings aren't hollow when seen from the side.

**Base** items
:   A flat plate covering the whole footprint, at a height within the row (**Origin**, from 0 at the bottom of the row to 1 at the top, plus **Offset Y**), optionally pushed out past the walls (**Expand**). Use them for floor slabs and flat ceilings. A floor builds its base plates once, even when several walls have them.

Both kinds have a **Material**, and **Flip Triangulation** to turn their faces around when they show the wrong side. Wall items are UV-mapped in meters, along the wall and across the profile, so a tiling texture keeps its real size.

**Editing a profile.** The **Profile** graph shows the cross-section beside the wall: X is how far out from the wall, in meters, and Y runs from the bottom (0) to the top (1) of the row. Pick a **Preset** (**Flat**, **Box**, **Parapet**, **Cornice**, **Chamfer** or **Pilaster**) and click **Apply** to start from a common shape. In the graph:

- **Drag** points to move them. Hold ++ctrl++ to snap in steps of 0.05.
- **Double-click** a span to add a point. **Right-click** a point, or press ++delete++, to remove it. Two points always stay.
- **Middle-drag** pans, the wheel zooms, and ++f++ or **Frame** fits the view.

The **Points** list below the graph holds the exact values.

### Corners

A corner closes the footprint at a knot. A wall's **Corner** section is used on every floor, at the start knot of each side that uses the wall. A row's own **Corner** replaces it on that row's floors. In other words, each knot is closed by the wall of the side that starts there. A corner trims both walls next to it, so the wall pieces stop where the corner begins.

**Convexity**
:   Which corners it accepts, seen from outside the building: **Any**, outside corners (**Convex**) or inside corners (**Concave**).

**Minimum Turn Angle**
:   Corners that turn less than this many degrees are left to the walls, for example the smooth knots of a curved footprint.

**Path**
:   The corner's shape seen from above. **Sharp** goes straight into the knot and out again, **Round** follows an arc tangent to both walls, and **Custom** follows your own **Custom Points**.
:   **Inset** is how much wall each side gives up to a **Sharp** or **Custom** corner. A **Round** corner uses **Radius** instead, and the trim follows from the radius and the corner angle. **Round Segments** sets how many pieces the arc is made of.
:   The plan view below shows the corner on a test angle, set with **Preview Angle**. With a **Custom** shape, drag its points there.

A corner is one of two kinds:

**Prefab** corners
:   Bend a straight prefab around the path. Model it as a flat wall piece with a vertical cut in the middle for **Sharp** paths (the cut lands on the knot), and with more vertical cuts for **Round** or **Custom** paths. The first piece that passes its chance roll is used.
:   The piece field warns when the prefab has no cut in the middle, or when its mesh isn't Read/Write enabled: it then bends in the editor but not in builds.

**Procedural** corners
:   Sweep one or more **Layers** along the path. Each layer has its own **Profile**, **Material**, offsets and **Smooth Shading Angle**, like a procedural wall item. **Bottom Margin** and **Top Margin** leave part of the floor height empty, so several layers can stack.

If some walls of a volume have a corner and others don't, the building inspector points it out: the knots where a wall without a corner starts stay open.

## :lucide-list-checks: Checking the result

The top of the Building inspector sums up the last generation: how many prefab pieces, procedural pieces, corners and roof pieces it made, on how many floors, and how long it took. Below that, it lists the problems it found, errors first. **Click** one to jump to the field that causes it. It reports, for example:

- a missing footprint, a volume with fewer than three corners, or a volume without walls,
- footprint sides that got no wall,
- a wall entry with no wall asset, a **Chance** of 0, or an **Explicit Knot Start** past the last knot,
- a row shorter than the floor it shares with another wall's row, which leaves a gap above it: give rows that share a floor the same height,
- volumes that overlap at the same height,
- **Single** slots that didn't fit their side, with the length they need and the length that was left,
- walls or roofs that have problems of their own: select the wall, or expand the roof, to see them.

Stretching filler slots that didn't fit a short side are only reported as information: the other slots stretched over them, so the wall stays closed.

### :lucide-scan-eye: Layout Overlay

With **Layout Overlay** on in the Buildings panel, selected buildings show their layout in the Scene view: each footprint side in its wall's color with the wall's name (**No wall** when it has none), the floor lines, the corner paths with a label on each knot of the first floor, and the row of each floor on the side closest to the camera.

- **Click** a wall label to give that side a different wall: one of the volume's walls, or any Volume Wall in the project under **Other Walls**. The volume's **Walls** list is rewritten as explicit runs, so every other side keeps its current wall. The menu can also **Open** the current wall asset. On a building with a **Rules Override**, the choices are disabled: change the rules instead.
- **Drag** the dots at the corners of the first floor along the wall to set the corner's **Inset**, or its **Radius** for round corners.
- **Drag** the handles at the top of procedural rows, on the side closest to the camera, up or down to set the row's **Height**.
- Hold ++ctrl+shift++ over a generated piece to see which wall, row, slot or item and floor it comes from, and **click** to open that field of the wall.

The corner and row height handles edit the Volume Wall asset, so every building that uses the wall follows.

### :wtk-component-building-generated-piece: Generated pieces

Every generated piece has a **Building Generated Piece** component. For pieces that come from a Volume Wall (prefab pieces, procedural items and corners), its inspector shows where they come from (**Generated by**), with **Open Source Field** to jump to that field. **Select Building** selects the piece's building.

!!! tip "Author with baking off"
    With **Auto Bake After Generation** on, the pieces are replaced by one baked mesh, so there's nothing left to hover or inspect. Turn it off while you work on a building's walls, and back on when you're done.
